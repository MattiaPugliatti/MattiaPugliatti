I am a space engineer working at the intersection of autonomy, perception, and deep space. Currently, I am serving as Head of On-Board Systems at Nautilus – Navigation in Space, an Italian SME developing ground-based navigation applications and onboard autonomous GNC systems.

My research centers on making spacecraft smarter about what they see. I embed data-driven image-processing algorithms directly onboard for autonomous navigation, segmentation, hazard detection, and shape recognition around small, irregular bodies such as comets and asteroids. These are environments where light, shape, and gravity are all unpredictable, and where a spacecraft often has to decide for itself what to do next. My interests span autonomous optical navigation, artificial intelligence, GNC, and small-bodies exploration.

My goal is to become a specialist in interplanetary missions and to design cutting-edge, autonomous, optical-based systems.

I have always liked a good challenge and with this mentality I have traveled the world as a space engineer, gaining valuable experiences both from a personal and professional standpoint. I have had the privilege of visiting and working in several locations, including Bremen (DFKI), Tucson (University of Arizona), Madrid (GMV), Tokyo (ISAS/JAXA), Friedrichshafen (Airbus Defense and Space), Delft (TU Delft), and my hometown of Milan (DART, Politecnico di Milano). To date, I have contributed to CubeSat (Milani, EQUULEUS), smallsat (DESTINY+), and traditional (HERA) missions, as well as research projects with national (ASI, ESA) and international (JAXA, NASA) space agencies at various capacities.

I’m always looking for new opportunities to expand my research and to work with experts from other fields. If you’re working on something in autonomous navigation, onboard perception, or small-body exploration, I’d love to hear from you.
